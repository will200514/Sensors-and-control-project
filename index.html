Abstract

A TurtleBot3 Burger in a MuJoCo room with randomly placed obstacles has to find a charging station marked with a single ArUco marker (ID 1, DICT_4X4_50) and dock in front of it. The robot is not told its start pose, the dock position or the obstacle positions. It rotates on the spot until the camera detects the marker, uses that first detection to define a dock-centred frame, then plans a path through a LiDAR occupancy grid and drives to a stopping point 0.30 m in front of the marker.

The state is the planar pose [x, y, theta]. An extended Kalman filter predicts with a unicycle model driven by wheel-encoder odometry and corrects with full-pose measurements recovered from the marker's four corners using PnP. A time-varying LQR controller, designed on the linearised tracking-error model, tracks the planned path using the EKF estimate only. Simulator ground truth is used only to score and plot results.

Model

The TurtleBot3 is modelled as a differential-drive unicycle with state x = [x, y, θ] and input u = [v, ω], where v = (vR + vL)/2 and ω = (vR − vL)/b with track width b = 0.160 m and wheel radius 0.033 m. The discrete motion model integrates a constant-(v, ω) arc over each step Δt = 0.05 s:

xk+1 = xk + vΔt·s·cos(θk + ωΔt/2),   yk+1 = yk + vΔt·s·sin(θk + ωΔt/2),   θk+1 = θk + ωΔt

with s = sin(a)/a, a = ωΔt/2, which removes the singularity at ω = 0. The Jacobians F = ∂f/∂x and G = ∂f/∂u are used by the EKF.

For control, the model is linearised about the reference trajectory in the robot-frame error e = [ex, ey, eθ] with correction input δu = [δv, δω]. The resulting (A, B) pair is discretised at 20 Hz. Controllability: the error model is controllable whenever the reference speed vr ≠ 0, which is checked in the unit tests; the tracker clamps vr to at least 0.01 m/s for this reason. Observability: the camera measures the full pose (H = I3), so the state is observable whenever the marker is in view. With odometry only, the absolute pose is not observable and the error grows without bound, which the odometry-only comparison in the results shows.

Coordinate frames

D (dock frame): origin on the floor directly below the marker centre, +x out of the wall, +y along the wall, +z up. It is created at the first accepted marker detection. R (robot frame): x forward, y left, z up; the EKF estimates the pose of R in D. C (camera frame): OpenCV convention, x right, y down, z forward, mounted 0.06 m ahead of and 0.12 m above the robot origin. Before acquisition the EKF runs in a frame defined by the robot's start pose; at acquisition the map is discarded and rebuilt in D. The docking target is [0.30, 0, π] in D, which is a marker-relative offset, not a room coordinate.

Sensing

  • Camera: 640x480 at 10 Hz, fx = 530 px, principal point (320, 240), zero distortion in simulation. Only marker ID 1 is accepted. Square-marker PnP gives candidate poses, then an upright-marker, level-robot fit picks the one with the lowest reprojection error (rejected above 2.0 px).
  • Measurement covariance: the corner-fit Jacobian maps an assumed corner noise of 1.0 px into a full 3x3 pose covariance, with floors of 1.5 cm and 1.0° for calibration and model error, then scaled by r_scale = 2.0. Near-ambiguous fits inflate the covariance.
  • Wheel encoders: 20 Hz, speed noise 0.003 m/s, 2% per-step slip and up to 1% per-trial bias.
  • LiDAR: 120 beams, 3.5 m range, σ = 0.01 m, at 5 Hz. Used for the occupancy grid only.

Evaluation and ablations

Docking error over 50 trials, NEES histogram against χ23, and success rate under ablations (noise scaling, gate off, ±5% focal length error, 30° FOV, ±5% wheel bias, 7 obstacles).

LQR trade-off

Scaling R from 0.01 to 100 trades control effort against tracking error. Tracking RMSE rises from about 8.2 cm to 12 cm as R increases, and success drops to about 83% at R × 100, where corrections are penalised so heavily that the robot drifts off the path. R × 1 was kept as a balance.

Results discussion and reflection

Both full runs docked within 3.1 cm and 1.3°, inside the 5 cm and 5° success criteria. The dock-phase RMSE is dominated by the first camera fix: a small marker seen from 4 to 5 m gives a noisy and sometimes ambiguous pose, and that error is carried into the dock frame until closer views correct it. Seed 3 went from 0.547 m RMSE over the dock phase to 2.94 cm at the end.

Mean NEES of 7.4 to 8.6 is above the ideal value of 3, so the filter is overconfident. Likely causes are rasterisation of the marker corners, planar-pose ambiguity at distance, and persistent wheel bias that a three-state filter does not estimate. We did not tune the noise down to make the plots look better; the noise scales are left explicit in src/config.py.

Limitations: the robot searches by rotating on the spot, so a fully occluded marker is never found and the run times out after 40 s. The scene generator keeps a line of sight to the marker for this reason. These are smoke tests rather than a full statistical study. Future work: estimate wheel bias as extra states, add exploration when the marker is not visible, and run the full evaluation sweep on the final code.
